# What the calculator is, seen against the field

**Date:** 2026-09-13
**Scope:** A short statement of what the p(doom) calculator is as an instrument,
now that the reports separate kept from moved rows and the literature has been
read beside it. Follows [the p(doom) literature](2026-09-13-pdoom-literature.md)
and the notes it links. Meant as the paragraph to reach for when the site has to
be described to someone, and as the list of what is still missing.
**Outcome:** The calculator is the only per-link elicitation of AI catastrophe risk
from the public, and the only one that has measured and published its own priming.
Its one real weakness is the sample; its undated question and its wide outcome
are design positions, and by 2026, with the first two links already visible in
the exhibits, the undated question is the better-framed one.
Three things are missing that the literature makes visible: a sentence on the
single-system assumption, one theory exhibit per link, and an About page that
explains the chain.

---

## 1. The only instrument in the field

Everything in the literature note is a survey, a decomposition done on paper, or
an argument. Carlsmith multiplied six numbers he chose himself. Growiec and
Prettner wrote a four-step formula and noted that its inputs had never been
elicited. Grace, Karger, Field and Kestin–Soares asked people for one number.

The calculator has been eliciting the per-link inputs from the public for a year.
For each submission it holds the three factors with their bands, the quiz answers,
whether the expert check was cleared and which terms were ticked, a signed
identity, the page version, and since 12 September the number the page proposed.
No survey in the list records what its respondents knew or what they were shown
before answering. And the calculator is the only one that measured its own
priming, on the same rows, and published the result: 50 of 149 quiz submissions
registered the page's proposal unmoved. The surveys can only wonder about theirs.

## 2. Weaknesses, and which are chosen

- **The sample cannot be described.** Anonymous, self-selected, reached through
  whatever links the site has. Every cohort median is where a group happened to
  sit, and the reports say so on every figure.
- **No time horizon.** The literature asks "by 2100" or "by 2070". The calculator
  asks unconditionally, which is simpler for a visitor and incomparable with
  everyone else. It is also, by 2026, the better-framed question. The surveys'
  horizons were set when the first link was the open one. The exhibits now show
  instances of the first two: a harness resolving Navier–Stokes faster than any
  human could, and a lab's agents executing code on a third party's servers. Those
  links have moved from prediction to extrapolation, and the whole of the remaining
  uncertainty sits on the third. What has not been observed is the second link at
  the scale that matters, dangerous behaviour outside an evaluation that nobody
  can reconstruct and contain; the Hugging Face intrusion was assigned, unguarded,
  and contained, as its exhibit says. So the relevant horizon is not when powerful
  AI arrives but how long the third link holds after the first two have visibly
  begun, and a question without a date is the right shape for that.
- **The outcome is wider than anyone else's.** "Global catastrophe" covers
  extinction, disempowerment and collapse together. This is deliberate and, for a
  chain, right: once the critical path is satisfied the outcome is bad whatever
  form it takes, and asking which would be a fourth link nobody can estimate. The
  cost is that the site's numbers compare only with the site's own earlier numbers.

The first is what surveys do well and the calculator does not. The second and
third are design decisions, made, and the reports should state them once.

## 3. The chain is doing real work

Three sliders that multiply force a visitor to say which link they doubt, and the
answer turned out to be structured rather than noise:

| Cohort, movers only | Doubts most |
|---|---|
| Before the quiz existed | every link about equally |
| Beginner | none strongly |
| Medium | the second, that powerful AI misbehaves |
| Cleared the check | the third, that misbehaviour becomes catastrophe |

Carlsmith put his doubt in the middle. Field's two camps do not contain the
check-clearers' chain. Kestin and Soares found the same movement direction, experts
down and newcomers up, on a different instrument. The calculator has a finding the
field does not have, at a sample size that makes it a hint, and the notes before
this one say why not to build around it yet.

## 4. Still missing

- **The single-system assumption.** The chain treats "AI" as one system whose
  dangerous behaviour either escalates or is contained. Dung's multipolar world,
  many systems and none dominant, is a survival route the sliders cannot express.
  Whether it needs a slider or a sentence is open; that it is assumed should be
  said somewhere a visitor reads.
- **One theory exhibit per link.** The second link has incidents and no argument.
  Turner or Ngo would give it one; Kovarik, as a stated limit, would serve the
  third. The exhibits file's sourcing rule accommodates papers.
- **An About page that explains the chain.** It is a reading list. It should be
  Carlsmith for why there is a chain, Grace for the reference number with the
  definitional caveat, and the chain's own arithmetic: the product is zero only if
  a link is, and each link is a strategy.

## 5. In one paragraph

A public, per-link elicitation of the probability that AI causes a global
catastrophe. Visitors set three conditional probabilities, powerful AI arrives,
it behaves dangerously, that becomes a catastrophe, and the product is their
number. A quiz proposes a starting point from what they know, the page records
that proposal and whether they moved it, and an expert path is gated by a
vocabulary check with decoys. Submissions are signed per browser. An evidence page
documents incidents for each link and requires every entry to state what it fails
to show. Reports are built from the export by a script that checks its own
assumptions, and they read their own results as skeptically as anyone else would.
The number on the front page is the least interesting thing the site produces.
