# Where the zero goes

**Date:** 2026-09-13
**Scope:** What the chain's arithmetic implies, which link the site's visitors put
their zero on, and whether the first link is already close to one. Follows
[experts cut the last link](2026-09-13-experts-cut-the-last-link.md) and
[the calculator against The Economics of p(doom)](2026-09-13-economics-of-pdoom.md).
**Outcome:** p(doom) is a product, so for humanity to be safe at least one link
has to be zero, and each link is a safety strategy. On this site nobody who has
taken a quiz has put any link at zero; the people who register near-zero do it on
the second or third link, never the first. The first link is close to one already,
on the evidence of a system, not a model, resolving Navier–Stokes in 88 hours;
what remains of it is the question of domains where no checker exists.

---

## 1. The arithmetic

The calculator computes p(doom) as the product of three sliders. A product is zero
only when a factor is zero. Every value on the site today is nonzero, so every
submission says humanity is not safe; the argument is only over how unsafe.

For the number to be zero, one link must be, and each link is the name of a
strategy:

| Link at zero | What has to be true | Who argues for it |
|---|---|---|
| P(powerful AI) | It never arrives: not built, or cannot be built | PauseAI and the campaign organisations on the expert quiz |
| P(dangerous \| powerful) | It arrives and never behaves dangerously: alignment or control solved | The alignment research programme; the "controllable tool" camp in Growiec and Prettner |
| P(catastrophe \| dangerous) | It behaves dangerously and the world absorbs it: detection, isolation, recovery | Incident response, evals, the exhibits' own `limits` fields |

Growiec and Prettner put a number on how small the product has to be: with
ordinary risk aversion, the tolerable immediate misalignment probability comes out
near 0.001%. The verified experts' chain of 0.95 × 0.80 × 0.50 is 38%, four orders
of magnitude away. Cutting one link in half, which is what the experts do, does not
approach safety by that standard. Only a zero does.

## 2. Which link the visitors zero

Of the 253 rows, 18 register a p(doom) at or below 1%. Fifteen of them predate the
quiz. Among the rows with an exact zero on some link:

| Link zeroed | Rows |
|---|---|
| First, P(powerful AI) | 1 |
| Second, P(dangerous \| powerful) | 7 |
| Third, P(catastrophe \| dangerous) | 8 |

(Five rows zero both the second and third, the 1 / 0 / 0 pattern from October
2025.) Since the quiz launched in March 2026, two rows have put a link at zero,
both medium-quiz takers and both on the second link. No expert has. The lowest
verified expert, at 0.4%, got there with 82%, 51% and 1%: a small third link, not
an empty one.

So on this site, the people who think humanity is safe think it is safe because
catastrophe can be contained, or because the system will not misbehave. Almost
nobody thinks it is safe because powerful AI will not come. That is consistent
with the previous note: the informed cohort's attention has moved off the first
link entirely.

## 3. The first link is close to one

The case that P(powerful AI) is already near its maximum rests on one exhibit: a
system resolved a Millennium Prize problem in 88 hours, with a machine-checked
proof. A single human mathematician, however gifted, needs sleep and food and would
take longer than 88 hours; the system is therefore already more capable than the
best single human on that task, and the best single human is above the baseline.

Two things about how that argument is stated, because they decide what it proves.

**"AI" means the system, not the model.** The result came from a harness running
on the order of 10,000 agents, cross-pollinated by a second model, with a third
model doing the Lean formalisation. Comparing that to one human is the right
comparison, because the human is a single instance too and cannot be run wide. A
system that turns one model into a century of agent-time in four days is the
deployed object, and it is the deployed object that the second and third links
have to contain. Objections of the form "but it took a hundred agent-years" miss
this: wall-clock and dollars are what matter for the chain, and on both the system
wins outright.

**The evidence is from the one domain with a checker.** Mathematics is where a
proof can be verified by Lean in 17 hours, so the search runs without a human in
the loop and the answer is known to be right. The second link is about dangerous
behaviour in the world, where no checker exists. Whether the capability transfers
to domains where nobody can verify the output is the whole of what remains of the
first link. It is a limit on the evidence, not on the system, and the verified
experts' 95% already prices it in. The next exhibit for this link should come from
somewhere a proof assistant cannot reach.

## 4. What this means for the instrument

- The chain's structure is doing real work. Three sliders that multiply force a
  visitor to say which link they believe is small, and the table shows that the
  answer has shifted: from "it won't come" in no one, to "it can be contained" in
  the informed cohort.
- The About page should say this. It currently explains nothing about why there
  are three sliders. One paragraph stating that the product is zero only if a
  factor is, and that each factor is a strategy, would do more for a new visitor
  than the reading list.
- A per-link version of the report's proposed-versus-registered figure would show
  directly which link each cohort moves and by how much. The stored proposal makes
  this a small addition.
- None of this changes the caution in the previous two notes. Nineteen verified
  experts and eighteen near-zero rows are direction, not size.
