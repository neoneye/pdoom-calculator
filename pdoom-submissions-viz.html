Tested hypothesis: does watching AI films predict p(doom)?

The idea was that people who have seen a lot of AI fiction would have richer scenarios in mind, and land somewhere different from people who have seen almost none. Only the beginner quiz asks this, so the test is 23 respondents. Left: how many of them ticked each title. Right: those same people split by how many titles they ticked, against the p(doom) they submitted.

Sorted by share who ticked it. * = option added partway through the period, so it was only offered to some respondents and its denominator is smaller.

Verdict: not supported. The three groups' medians land within 3 points of each other (A 68%, B 71%, C 70%) while each group internally spans 70+ points. Films seen vs p(doom) is r = −0.21 (ρ = −0.17) — noise at this sample size. Neither does uncertainty separate them: the median range-width is 0.51 / 0.45 / 0.51, and what looks like a wider group A is three individuals, not a group trait.

Second hypothesis: does knowing more risk types move p(doom)?

Same test, same 23 beginners, different checklist: the 12 catastrophe types they say they recognise. Left: how many recognised each. Middle: split by how many they recognised, against p(doom). Right: risks recognised against how wide they drew their own ranges.

Verdict: not on p(doom) — but two things do show. The group medians are non-monotonic (A 73%, B 53%, C 72%): the middle group is lowest, which is what a non-effect looks like. Pearson r = −0.26 against Spearman ρ = −0.09 confirms it — the linear number is carried by a few extreme values, not a trend. What does hold: the least-aware group contains no skeptics at all (group C's minimum is 48%, while A and B both hold sub-10% submissions), and recognising more risks goes with narrower ranges (r = −0.40; dropping any single respondent leaves it between −0.32 and −0.51). Note the group medians for width look cleaner than the data — group A's widths are bimodal — so the correlation is the trustworthy statistic, not the group median.

What the data says

  • Submitted p(doom) has drifted sharply upward. The median rose from 15% in the first three months (Oct–Dec 2025, n=83) to 68% in the last three (Jun–Aug 2026, n=39). Whether visitors got gloomier or the audience changed can't be separated from this data.
  • Beginners report the highest numbers, experts the lowest of the tagged groups. Median 70% for beginner-quiz takers (n=23), 67% for medium (n=19), 56% for expert (n=18). All three tagged groups sit well above the untagged early crowd (median 14%, n=102) — but the tags only exist in the later, higher period, so level and era are entangled.
  • The extremes are used. 10 submissions put the midpoint at exactly 0% and 2 at ≈100%; both ends persist across the whole period.
  • Traffic is bursty. Oct 2025 (n=55, launch) and Aug 2026 (n=25) dominate; Jan 2026 had only 4 submissions, so single months with small n (Jan, Jul) swing easily.
  • Everyone believes the first link; the verdict is decided at the last. The median P(powerful AI) is 86% across all submissions, but correlation with the final p(doom) grows down the chain: r = 0.55 for P(powerful AI), 0.73 for P(dangerous behavior), 0.81 for P(global catastrophe).
  • Quiz engagement doesn't predict doom. Beginners tick the most boxes (median 26 selections vs 15.5 for experts), but the number of selections is uncorrelated with p(doom) (r ≈ −0.06). Among beginners, having seen more AI films is weakly negatively related to p(doom) (r = −0.21, n = 23 — noise-level).
  • Film exposure is mass-market, not AI-canon — which may be why it predicts nothing. The Matrix (21/23) and Terminator (20/23) are near-universal, but Ex Machina — the film most cited in alignment discourse — was ticked by 2 of 23, and Pantheon, Transcendence, Neuromancer and Murderbot by nobody at all. "Films seen" here largely measures general movie-watching.
  • Risk awareness buys precision, not doom. Recognising more catastrophe types leaves p(doom) where it is, but goes with visibly tighter ranges (r = −0.40) — the opposite of the "the more you know, the less sure you are" intuition. It also appears to be a precondition for low estimates: nobody in the least-aware group submitted below 48%. Film exposure and risk awareness are essentially unrelated (r = +0.10), so they measure different things.
  • Uncertainty stays wide. Individual p10–p90 ranges typically span 30–50 percentage points — visitors express low confidence regardless of where their midpoint sits.
